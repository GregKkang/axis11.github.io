How the market response compared with historical patterns

The S&P 500 fell 0.38% on Tuesday, while the fixed model’s reading of the day’s news was +0.10%. The market finished 0.48 percentage points below that estimate. The model missed the direction, but the gap was ordinary by its own historical standard. About half of the eligible sessions had an absolute error at least this large, and Tuesday’s return was inside both error bands.

The news mix included an upside surprise in both headline and core inflation, a Fed governor’s indication that a larger December rate increase was possible, and continuing uncertainty around Nvidia and data-center investment. The model mapped those facts to a near-flat aggregate return. CNBC’s account described a weaker session, especially for the Nasdaq. The index-level regression cannot isolate how much of the decline came from rates, Nvidia or other developments. A gap of this size gives little basis for calling the response excessive.

Text used by the model

The briefing author used generative extraction and checked the facts against CNBC’s closing narrative. The text was locked before inference. Realized stock and index returns, Treasury yield moves, and rate expectations tied to contemporaneous repricing were excluded. The rolling liveblog entries were outside the model’s source scope. The weekend Nvidia development is prior information for Tuesday.

October headline U.S. consumer prices rose 0.4% from the prior month, above the 0.3% expected by economists polled by Dow Jones.
October U.S. core consumer prices rose 0.3% from the prior month, above economists’ 0.2% expectation.
The October CPI release omitted annual figures because of the 2025 government shutdown.
Fed Governor Christopher Waller said in London on Tuesday that a December rate increase was likely and that a half-percentage-point increase was possible amid persistent inflation.
U.S. Bank Asset Management strategist Tom Hainlin said the October CPI report suggested inflation could stay elevated longer and the Fed might need to tighten more aggressively in 2027.
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang acknowledged over the weekend a relationship that CNBC linked to allegations of foreign intelligence activity.
Following strong Democratic midterm results, investors were concerned about a potential slowdown in data-center construction.

CNBC’s browser displays gave conflicting November 10 and November 11 update dates. The first-publication time and the version available at the close could not be independently verified. The audit preserves the retrieved narrative and both update labels.

The fixed model uses local BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings and Ridge alpha=1, fitted on 750 sessions in 2023–2025. The saved model hash was verified before inference.

The bands use 211 out-of-training evaluation residuals from 2026 through November 3. The excluded exchange sessions are November 4, November 5, November 6, November 9 and November 10. The historical frequency uses the same sample.

The 80% and 95% half-widths use ranks ceil((211+1)×0.80)=170 and ceil((211+1)×0.95)=202 of the sorted absolute log errors. The half-widths are 0.0097979462 and 0.0172323218. They are added to and subtracted from the estimated log return before conversion to simple returns. The bands describe historical reconstruction errors.
