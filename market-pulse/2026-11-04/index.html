Post-election relief outpaces the historical model

How the market response compared with historical patterns

The S&P 500 returned +1.58%, against a model estimate of +0.40%. The actual return was 1.18 percentage points above the estimate based on historical news relationships.

The model associated the combined non-price news with a modest positive session. The market delivered a substantially larger gain. On this comparison, the day’s response was more favorable than the historical model reconstructed from the text; this was not simply a routine match between the estimated and actual move.

An absolute log-return gap at least this large occurred on 11.6% of the 207 eligible evaluation days. The actual return was above the historical 80% upper band of +1.39%, but within the 95% band. The gap merits review without being treated as an unprecedented outcome.

One possible interpretation is that resolution of political uncertainty mattered more to investors than the model’s text representation captured. The same input also retained policy and regulatory risks, so the estimate did not assume uniformly favorable consequences. This is a hypothesis about the residual, not a measured causal contribution. The regression cannot separate the effects of election certainty, diplomatic expectations and positioning, or establish that prices overshot fair value. A larger-than-estimated gain is not, by itself, proof of overreaction.

CNBC recap and supporting context

CNBC described a broad post-election rally after Democrats won Congress and Trump accepted the result. Expectations of pressure to end the Iran war accompanied lower oil prices. Data-center restrictions remained a concern even as economically sensitive shares advanced.

CNBC closing recap

Text used by the model

The briefing author performed generative extraction and same-assistant source-context checks under the existing instructions. This is not a fresh Claude response or an independent review. The source scope remains the complete CNBC closing narrative used by the frozen dataset; ancillary rolling-blog entries are not appended. Asset-price moves, rotation, price forecasts and descriptions of bullish sentiment were excluded. The seven event summaries below were locked before model inference.

Democrats secured majorities in both chambers of Congress and won key gubernatorial races, including Texas.
President Trump said he would accept the election results and that he and Republicans would work with Democrats.
Vice President JD Vance attributed Republican election losses to the Iran war.
A One Point BFG Wealth Partners investment chief expected the election result to increase pressure to end the Iran war.
Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa had pledged an immediate moratorium on data centers in the state, adding to concerns about restrictions on the AI infrastructure buildout.
The incoming Democratic Congress would check an administration that had enacted business-friendly tax cuts while pursuing tariffs and the Iran war, which the article linked to inflation.
Commentators expected divided government and legislative gridlock, limiting the scope for policy and regulatory change.

Calculation

100 × (exp(b+w·x) − 1) = +0.398983%

The model remains fixed: 750 training sessions from 2023–2025, Ridge alpha=1, and unchanged local BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings. No coefficients were refitted.

Error-band calibration for this briefing: 207 validated, out-of-training evaluation sessions in 2026 through October 28. The five excluded trading sessions are October 29, October 30, November 2, November 3 and November 4: the target day and its four immediately preceding sessions. No target-day or future residual enters calibration. The sample expands as eligible validated observations become available.

The absolute log-return errors are sorted. The 80% and 95% half-widths use ranks ceil((207+1)×0.80)=167 and ceil((207+1)×0.95)=198, giving 0.0097756092 and 0.0172323218 in log-return units. Each half-width is added to and subtracted from the model’s log estimate, then converted with 100×(exp(x)−1). The historical frequency above uses this same sample.

These are descriptive historical error bands, not fair-value ranges, causal estimates or guaranteed coverage intervals. Earlier briefings retain their original calibration. Historical model and source notes.
