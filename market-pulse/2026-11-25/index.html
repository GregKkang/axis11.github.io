How the market response compared with historical patterns

The S&P 500 returned -0.25% against a model estimate of -0.14%. The gap was -0.12 percentage points. The model and the S&P 500 both registered a modest decline, with a gap of about 0.12 percentage points. The close was comfortably inside the 80% band. That agreement concerns the close-to-close return only: a single daily estimate cannot explain the much larger intraday decline or its subsequent reversal, nor show that the market correctly priced each inflation or tariff development.

An absolute log-return error at least this large occurred on 86.94% of the same eligible calibration sample. This compares same-day outcomes with historical news–return relationships; it is not a future-return forecast or a causal fair-value estimate.

CNBC recap and supporting context

CNBC reported a sharp intraday reversal that left the Dow higher but the S&P 500 and Nasdaq lower. Its nonprice account included disappointing Ambarella results and guidance, persistent PCE inflation, expectations for further Fed tightening and new U.S. tariff measures. The late recovery was also associated with light holiday trading and Michael Burry’s disclosed positioning; those trading-related explanations were excluded from the model input.

FRED confirmed all three preceding closes and the current Dow close. The retrieved S&P 500 and Nasdaq FRED observations still ended on November 24; their November 25 closes agree between CNBC’s closing narrative and closing update. CNBC closing recap · FRED S&P 500 · FRED Dow · FRED Nasdaq

Text used by the model

The briefing author performed generative extraction and same-assistant source checks. This differs from the original Claude extractor used for training and is not independent review. The complete featured closing narrative was used; rolling updates were outside the fixed input scope. The input was locked before inference, and realized prices, returns, positioning and market-response commentary were removed. Reported opinions retain their attribution. Earlier events are background, not newly announced events.

Ambarella’s current-quarter forecast was weak, and its third-quarter earnings and revenue missed expectations.
October headline PCE inflation was 0.3% monthly and 3.8% annually, both as expected; annual inflation increased from 3.7% in September.
October core PCE inflation, excluding food and energy, was 3.3% annually, slightly above the Dow Jones forecast.
The PCE release reinforced expectations for Federal Reserve rate increases in December and January.
The White House unveiled new tariffs on more than a dozen major trading partners, including China and the European Union.
Nvidia had ousted and subsequently reinstated Jensen Huang, and Democrats had swept the midterm elections.

CNBC displayed an update time of November 25 at 16:17 EST, after the regular close. The article was retrieved retrospectively; its first-publication time and exact version available at the regular close have not been verified.

Rolling-blog items including Nvidia’s CreditWatch placement, satellite authorization and the consumer-spending survey were outside the fixed closing-narrative scope. Trading volume, positioning and the price rebound itself were deliberately excluded as reaction-related information.

The model remains fixed: local BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings and Ridge alpha=1, fitted on 750 sessions in 2023–2025. Model and embedding asset hashes were verified; no refitting or paid API calls were used.

Calibration uses 222 out-of-training 2026 sessions through November 18. Excluded sessions: November 19, November 20, November 23, November 24, November 25. These are the target session and its four preceding NYSE sessions, not the last five available data rows. The extreme-error frequency uses the same calibration sample.

The absolute log-error order-statistic ranks are ceil((222+1)×0.80)=179 and ceil((222+1)×0.95)=212. Their half-widths are 0.0102657592 and 0.0181100313.
