A modest S&P gain tracks the model’s news reading

How the market response compared with historical patterns

The S&P 500 returned +0.31%, against a model estimate of +0.23%. The actual return was 0.09 percentage points above the estimate based on historical news relationships.

The input combined stronger employment data with continuing uncertainty about AI infrastructure policy and commercial relationships. Taken together, those facts were associated with a modest positive return in the historical model. The observed gain was slightly larger, but the difference was small: absolute log-return errors at least this large occurred on 90.9% of the 209 eligible evaluation days.

At the aggregate index level, this was a close reconstruction rather than an unusually strong response to the extracted news. It offers little basis for an underreaction or overreaction claim. The model does not identify which fact supplied how much of the gain, and the favorable net estimate does not imply that every input was favorable.

The cross-index differences are also relevant. The Nasdaq gained more than the S&P 500 while the Dow declined. A single S&P regression does not explain that dispersion or the contribution of individual semiconductor companies. Nor does Friday’s small model error resolve Thursday’s much larger miss. The weights were unchanged, and the recent-session exclusion rule keeps Thursday’s error out of today’s calibration sample.

Text used by the model

The briefing author performed generative extraction and same-assistant source-context checks under the existing instructions. This is not a fresh Claude response or an independent review. The source scope remains the complete CNBC closing narrative used by the frozen dataset; ancillary rolling-blog updates are not appended. Actual price moves, dip-buying, investor flows, weekly performance and post-correction price forecasts were excluded. The six summaries below were locked before inference. Category tags and the separate numeric-surprise fields were not passed to the embedding model.

Texas Governor-Elect Gina Hinojosa said Thursday evening that data-center development should pause until laws protecting Texans were in place, following Democratic calls to curb the buildout.
A MAI Capital Management strategist expected policy uncertainty to persist until incoming officials took office in January, but expected AI infrastructure deals eventually to be completed despite added headwinds.
Microsoft was planning to reduce the number of its neocloud partners, according to people familiar with the matter cited by CNBC.
U.S. nonfarm payrolls increased by 165,000 in October, compared with a Dow Jones economist-survey expectation of 59,000.
The U.S. unemployment rate was 4.1%, below the 4.2% rate economists had expected.

Calculation

Intercept b = -0.0006704786
Weighted embedding sum w·x = +0.0029558972
Estimated log return b+w·x = +0.0022854186
100 × (exp(b+w·x) − 1) = +0.228803%

The model remains fixed: 750 training sessions from 2023–2025, Ridge alpha=1, and unchanged local BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings. No coefficients were refitted.

Error-band calibration for this briefing: 209 validated, out-of-training evaluation sessions in 2026 through October 30. The five excluded trading sessions are November 2, November 3, November 4, November 5 and November 6: the target session and its four immediately preceding sessions. No target-day or future residual enters calibration.

Absolute log-return errors are sorted. The 80% and 95% half-widths use ranks ceil((209+1)×0.80)=168 and ceil((209+1)×0.95)=200, giving 0.0097756092 and 0.0172323218 in log-return units. Each half-width is added to and subtracted from the model’s log estimate, then converted with 100×(exp(x)−1). The historical frequency above uses this same sample.

These are descriptive historical error bands, not causal estimates, fair-value ranges or guaranteed coverage intervals. Earlier briefings retain their original calibration. Historical model and source notes.
