AI sell-off far exceeds the model’s near-flat estimate

Trading session: November 5, 2026 · 5 min read

How the market response compared with historical patterns

The S&P 500 returned -4.36%, against a model estimate of -0.01%—effectively unchanged. The actual return was 4.35 percentage points below the estimate based on historical news relationships.

This was a substantial failure to reconstruct the magnitude of the session. The extracted policy and earnings news produced an almost neutral estimate, while the market recorded a sharp decline. The estimate’s tiny negative sign offers little explanatory value when the realized loss was more than four percentage points larger.

The actual return was below both historical error bands, including the -1.71% lower limit of the 95% band. None of the 208 eligible evaluation days had an absolute log-return error as large as this one. That is a description of the observed comparison sample, not a claim that such a move had zero probability.

The defensible reading is that the market’s response was much more adverse than the fixed model’s historical text relationships reconstructed. CNBC’s account emphasized the implications of restrictions on AI infrastructure. The model retained that concern, yet did not translate it into anything close to the observed index decline. This points to a limitation in the text representation, the fitted relationship, or both; it does not establish that investors overreacted.

Coverage also matters. The closing narrative yielded four non-price summaries, concentrated on data-center policy, AI capital spending and Qualcomm’s mixed earnings update. They contain no quantified estimate of a reduction in investment and do not measure leverage, positioning or the exposure of the index’s largest constituents. The model therefore cannot determine how much of the residual came from a reassessment of fundamentals, omitted information or trading dynamics. It supplies an index-level comparison, not an event-by-event attribution or a fair-value verdict.

CNBC recap and supporting context

CNBC reported a technology-led decline that spread across the market and erased the previous day’s post-election gains. Concerns about constraints on data-center development weighed on AI-related companies. Qualcomm’s earnings beat came alongside a disappointing outlook.

CNBC closing recap

CNBC’s closing narrative supplies all three current index levels. FRED confirmed the Dow close; its retrieved S&P 500 and Nasdaq pages still ended on November 4, so November 5 closes for those two indexes remain CNBC-only in this release. The prior-session closes used for all three return calculations were independently checked on the FRED pages and match the preceding briefing. Earlier briefings have not been rewritten.

Text used by the model

The briefing author performed generative extraction and same-assistant source-context checks under the existing instructions. This is not a fresh Claude response or an independent review. The source scope remains the complete CNBC closing narrative used by the frozen dataset; ancillary rolling-blog entries are not appended. Actual prices, returns, investor flows, rotation and sell-off descriptions were excluded from the embedding input. The following four summaries were locked before inference.

Democratic victories in Congress and key gubernatorial races raised concerns that tighter restrictions could slow data-center construction and the AI infrastructure buildout.
Senator Bernie Sanders called for a nationwide data-center moratorium and reiterated his opposition in a Wednesday-evening social-media post.
Qualcomm reported earnings above analyst estimates but issued a weaker-than-expected earnings outlook.
Orion’s investment chief described AI capital spending as a major economic tailwind over roughly three years and highlighted uncertainty about the incoming party’s federal and state policy stance.

This release archives a rendered-text snapshot of the closing narrative. The page title and narrative identify the November 5 session, but the browser’s update-date labels were inconsistent, so an exact publication or revision timestamp has not been certified. This is a revised page, not a verified point-in-time archive. Sanders’s Wednesday-evening statement is prior information for Thursday. A call for a moratorium is not treated as enacted law, and concern about the buildout is not treated as a measured spending cut. Qualcomm’s stronger reported earnings and weaker outlook are both retained.

Calculation

Intercept b = -0.0006704786
Weighted embedding sum w·x = +0.0006167558
Estimated log return b+w·x = -0.0000537228
100 × (exp(b+w·x) − 1) = -0.005372%

The model remains fixed: 750 training sessions from 2023–2025, Ridge alpha=1, and unchanged local BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings. No coefficients were refitted.

Error-band calibration for this briefing: 208 validated, out-of-training evaluation sessions in 2026 through October 29. The five excluded sessions are October 30, November 2, November 3, November 4 and November 5: the target day and its four immediately preceding trading sessions. No target-day or future residual enters calibration.

Absolute log-return errors are sorted. The 80% and 95% half-widths use ranks ceil((208+1)×0.80)=168 and ceil((208+1)×0.95)=199, giving 0.0097756092 and 0.0172323218 in log-return units. Each half-width is added to and subtracted from the model’s log estimate, then converted with 100×(exp(x)−1). The historical error comparison above uses this same sample.

These are descriptive historical error bands, not causal estimates, fair-value ranges or guaranteed coverage intervals. Earlier briefings retain their original calibration. Historical model and source notes.
