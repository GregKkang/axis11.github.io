How the market response compared with historical patterns

The S&P 500 returned -3.70% against a model estimate of -0.18%. The gap was -3.52 percentage points. The model mapped the extracted nonprice news to a modest decline of about 0.18%. The actual fall was much larger: 3.70%, leaving a negative gap of 3.52 percentage points. It also fell well below the 95% historical error band. Only one of the 219 eligible evaluation sessions had an absolute log-return error at least as large. This was an unusually poor reconstruction of the session, rather than evidence that the market should have fallen by only 0.18%. The input preserves the reported regulatory, policy and AI-investment context, but deliberately excludes the sell-off itself, profit-taking, defensive rotation and asset-price signals.

On 0.46% of eligible evaluation days, the model’s absolute log-return error was at least this large. The gap measures what the model missed at the index level. It does not establish that the market overreacted or underreacted to a particular event.

CNBC recap and supporting context

CNBC reported another severe decline in AI-related stocks. The S&P 500 and Dow closed in correction territory, while the Nasdaq erased its gain for the year. The recap cited Reuters reporting about a possible SEC review of Nvidia accounting, moderation in AI investment, the change in Nvidia leadership and the risk of further Fed rate increases. These are reported facts and risks; their individual effects cannot be identified from the index return.

FRED confirmed all three November 19 comparison closes and the November 20 Dow close. Current S&P 500 and Nasdaq closes agree across CNBC’s closing narrative and its 16:17 EST update; the retrieved FRED series for those two indexes still ended on November 19. Text used by the model

The briefing author performed generative extraction and same-assistant checks against the complete closing narrative. This extractor differs from the original Claude extraction used to construct the training data. The input was locked before inference. Anthropic’s Thursday listing, the midterm outcome and Nvidia leadership change are prior information. The possible SEC accounting review is attributed to Reuters; it is not presented as a finding of wrongdoing. Saperstein’s description of AI financing and earnings conditions is attributed commentary, not independently verified data. The source does not establish its original timing. Realized prices, returns, market positioning, and rolling liveblog entries were excluded.

The ancillary liveblog also covered University of Michigan consumer sentiment and inflation expectations, Fed Governor Lisa Cook’s inflation warning and President Trump’s proposed restrictions on overseas data centers. Those items were outside the fixed closing-narrative input scope and were not added to this estimate. This is a material coverage limitation: the residual can reflect missing news as well as nonlinear reactions, trading dynamics and model error. It cannot establish overreaction or isolate the effect of any one event. This is a retrospective same-day explanation exercise, not a forecast of future returns or a causal fair-value estimate.

Calculation

Intercept b = -0.0006704786
Weighted embedding sum w·x = -0.0011698088
Estimated log return b+w·x = -0.0018402874
100 × (exp(b+w·x) − 1) = -0.183860%

The fixed model uses local BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings and Ridge alpha=1 fitted on 750 sessions in 2023–2025. The model hash was verified before inference.

Calibration uses 219 out-of-training 2026 sessions through November 13. The excluded sessions are November 16, November 17, November 18, November 19, November 20, the target day and four immediately preceding NYSE trading sessions. The frequency and bands use this same sample.

The 80% and 95% half-widths are the sorted absolute log-return errors at ranks ceil((219+1)×0.80)=176 and ceil((219+1)×0.95)=209. The half-widths are 0.0099470498 and 0.0180007499. Add and subtract each from the model log estimate, then convert the endpoints to simple returns using 100×(exp(x)−1). The bands describe historical errors in reconstructing same-day returns.
