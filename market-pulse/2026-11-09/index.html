A broad rebound runs well ahead of the news model

How the market response compared with historical patterns

Monday’s +2.35% S&P 500 return was substantially stronger than the fixed model’s -0.09% reading of the non-price news. The difference was +2.44 percentage points, placing the actual return above the upper end of the historical 95% error band.

The extracted facts described continuing uncertainty over data-center regulation, a reported Nvidia board investigation, and the week’s earnings and inflation calendar. In the historical regression, that combination was associated with a nearly flat, slightly negative return. It did not reconstruct the scale or direction of Monday’s advance. Only 2 of the 210 eligible evaluation sessions (0.95%) had absolute log-return errors at least this large.

CNBC’s account emphasized a broad technology rebound and falling oil prices. Those observations help describe the session, but realized asset-price moves and rebound commentary are excluded from the model input under the existing leakage rules. The distinction matters here: the model tests how well the extracted event facts map to historical returns; it does not absorb the observed oil decline or the equity rebound as explanatory inputs. Their exclusion limits what this particular reconstruction can capture.

The result is a large positive unexplained residual, rather than evidence that the market necessarily overreacted. The model cannot determine whether prices were justified, allocate the gap to a specific event, or separate changing positioning from omitted information. Friday’s much closer fit does not carry over to Monday. Both sessions use the same coefficients.

Text used by the model

The author used generative extraction and same-assistant source-context checks under the existing instructions. This was neither a fresh Claude response nor an independent review. The complete closing narrative remains the source scope; ancillary liveblog posts were not appended. All asset prices, returns, relative performance, rebound/oversold descriptions and price forecasts were excluded. The following six event summaries were locked before inference; audit metadata and evidence quotations were not embedded.

Following strong Democratic midterm election results, investors were concerned that tighter regulation of data-center construction, energy use and development costs could slow AI infrastructure expansion.
Nvidia announced Sunday that its board was opening an investigation into co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang’s relationship with a businesswoman whom U.S. intelligence agencies described as a Chinese spy.
Infrastructure Capital Advisors CEO Jay Hatfield expected Democratic policy to create obstacles in the near term and beyond 2028, but believed U.S. data centers would ultimately be built.
Jay Hatfield identified the Middle East conflict and the Federal Reserve’s interest-rate outlook as continuing sources of economic uncertainty.
CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials were scheduled to report earnings during the week.
The consumer price index was scheduled for release Tuesday and the producer price index Friday, with investors seeking information relevant to the Federal Reserve’s next decision.

The Nvidia investigation is a reported board action; the intelligence allegation is attributed, not presented as an adjudicated fact. Sunday’s announcement and the prior election outcome are prior information for Monday. The earnings and inflation releases remain scheduled events, not realized results. The source gives no new Fed decision to insert into the input.

Source-time limitation: the article identifies Monday’s session, but the browser displayed an update label of Tuesday, November 10, 2026 at 4:26 p.m. Eastern—later than this snapshot’s retrieval on November 9 UTC. The model is unchanged: 750 training sessions from 2023–2025, local BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings and Ridge alpha=1. The saved model hash was verified, and no coefficients were refitted.

Error-band calibration: 210 validated, out-of-training 2026 evaluation sessions through November 2. The five excluded exchange sessions are November 3, November 4, November 5, November 6 and November 9: the target session and its four preceding trading sessions. The November 7–8 weekend does not count toward the exclusion. No target-day, future or training-fit residual enters calibration.

Sorted absolute log errors supply ranks ceil((210+1)×0.80)=169 and ceil((210+1)×0.95)=201. The respective half-widths are 0.0097979462 and 0.0172323218. They are added to and subtracted from the estimated log return and then converted using 100×(exp(x)−1).
