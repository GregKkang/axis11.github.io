How the market response compared with historical patterns

The S&P 500 returned -2.02% against a model estimate of -0.14%. The gap was -1.88 percentage points. The historical mapping of the extracted facts was slightly negative, but it captured only a small part of the observed decline. The S&P 500 closed below both error bands, including the 95% lower endpoint of about −1.93%. Seven of the 218 eligible evaluation days had absolute log-return errors at least this large. The input preserves Anthropic’s listing, the offering outcome and the AMD allegations while excluding the IPO’s trading performance, investor selling and realized moves in other assets. Those exclusions matter on a day whose closing narrative emphasized a reversal in the AI trade.

On 3.21% of eligible evaluation days, the model’s absolute log-return error was at least this large. The gap measures what the model missed at the index level. It does not establish that the market overreacted or underreacted to a particular event.

CNBC recap and supporting context

CNBC described a sharp reversal as Anthropic began trading. All three major indexes closed roughly 2% lower. The closing account also highlighted allegations about AMD export controls and continuing concerns over whether AI infrastructure spending would produce sufficient profits.

FRED confirmed all three November 18 comparison closes and the November 19 Dow close. Current S&P 500 and Nasdaq closes agree across CNBC’s narrative and closing update, while their retrieved FRED pages still ended on November 18. CNBC closing recap · FRED S&P 500 · FRED Dow · FRED Nasdaq

Text used by the model

The briefing author performed generative extraction and same-assistant checks against the complete closing narrative. This extractor differs from the original Claude extraction used to construct the training data. The input was locked before inference. Anthropic’s offering outcome is prior information for Thursday. Its listing shortly before noon is a same-session event. The input excludes the offer price and aftermarket price performance. The AMD export-control claim is attributed to Hunterbrook Capital as an allegation. Realized prices, returns, market positioning, and rolling liveblog entries were excluded.

Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT shortly before noon on Thursday.
Anthropic completed its initial public offering above the expected offering range.
Hunterbrook Capital alleged that AMD had breached U.S. export controls involving China.
Investors were questioning whether heavy investment in AI infrastructure would translate into meaningful profits.
Nvidia was managing the departure of CEO Jensen Huang.

CNBC displayed conflicting November 19 and November 20 update labels. The first-publication time, modification time and version available at the regular close remain unverified.

The ancillary liveblog covered jobless claims, Philadelphia manufacturing data, Texas permitting, retail earnings and Gulf conflict reports. These were outside the fixed model’s closing-narrative source scope. The resulting coverage limitation matters when interpreting the residual. The large gap alone cannot establish overreaction or the causal effect of the IPO.

The fixed model uses local BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5 embeddings and Ridge alpha=1 fitted on 750 sessions in 2023–2025. The model hash was verified before inference.

Calibration uses 218 out-of-training 2026 sessions through November 12. The excluded sessions are November 13, November 16, November 17, November 18, November 19, the target day and four immediately preceding NYSE trading sessions. The frequency and bands use this same sample.

The 80% and 95% half-widths are the sorted absolute log-return errors at ranks ceil((218+1)×0.80)=176 and ceil((218+1)×0.95)=209. The half-widths are 0.0101068082 and 0.0181100313. Add and subtract each from the model log estimate, then convert the endpoints to simple returns using 100×(exp(x)−1). The bands describe historical errors in reconstructing same-day returns.
